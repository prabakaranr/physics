Reflection and Scattering of Light

A simple, interactive look at how light bounces off surfaces (mirrors vs. walls) and how tiny particles scatter light to paint our daytime sky blue and our sunsets red.

1. Reflection: The Law of Bouncing Light

Whenever light hits a boundary, it bounces off according to a simple universal rule:

Angle of Incidence (θi) = Angle of Reflection (θr)
Measured relative to the Normal (a perpendicular 90° line to the surface).

🪞 Specular Reflection (Mirror)

On a flat, smooth surface, all parallel incoming rays reflect in the exact same direction. This preserves the image and creates a clear mirror reflection.

📄 Diffuse Reflection (Wall, Paper, Table)

Microscopic bumps bounce rays in all random directions. You don't see your reflection, but you can see the object from any angle!

2. Scattering: Why Is the Sky Blue & Sunsets Red?

Scattering happens when light hits particles (like air molecules or dust) and gets deflected in all directions.

The 1/λ⁴ Rayleigh Rule: Blue Scatters 10× More than Red!
Tiny air molecules (N₂ and O₂) are much smaller than light wavelengths. The amount of scattering is proportional to 1 / λ⁴ (wavelength to the fourth power):

• Short blue wavelengths (~400 nm) scatter roughly 10 times more than long red wavelengths (~700 nm).
• During the day: Scattered blue photons fill the whole sky, making it appear bright blue from every direction!
• At sunset: Sunlight must travel through ~10× more atmosphere to reach you. Almost all the blue light scatters away before it reaches your eyes, leaving only the surviving red and orange rays!

3. Why Are Clouds White? (Mie Scattering)

Water droplets in clouds and fog are much larger than light wavelengths.

Unlike tiny air molecules, cloud droplets scatter all colors of light equally. When Red, Green, and Blue scatter together in equal amounts, your eyes combine them to see pure white!

Type of Scattering Particle Size What It Does Everyday Example
Rayleigh Scattering Smaller than wavelength (air molecules) Scatters short blue wavelengths ~10× more than red Blue daytime sky, Red sunsets
Mie Scattering Equal to or larger than wavelength (water drops, dust) Scatters all visible colors equally White clouds, fog, milk
