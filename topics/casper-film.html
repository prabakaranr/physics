Casper Cloaking Film: The Physics of Smart Privacy Glass

Applied to office conference room glass, Casper film does something that looks like science fiction: you can see people, tables, and whiteboards crystal-clearly, but computer monitors and TV screens appear pitch-black!

2. How Does It Work? (Wave Polarization)

Light is a transverse wave that wiggles perpendicular to its travel direction. The secret to Casper Cloaking Film relies on one crucial difference between screens and real-world objects:

📱 1. Screens Emit Polarized Light

Every LCD computer monitor and TV uses polarizing filters to create images. Therefore, 100% of light coming from screens is linearly polarized (all light waves oscillate along one specific angle).

🧍 2. Objects Reflect Unpolarized Light

Light bouncing off people, walls, paper, and coffee cups is unpolarized. Its waves wiggle randomly in every possible direction (vertical, horizontal, diagonal).

The Cross-Polarizer Trick (Malus's Law: I = I₀ cos²θ)
Casper film is engineered with an optical polarizing filter oriented at 90° relative to screen light:

• For the Screen: Since the angle difference is θ = 90°, Malus's Law dictates:
    I = I₀ · cos²(90°) = 0 → 100% of the screen's light is blocked!

• For People & Furniture: Because their light oscillates in all random directions, half of it passes through regardless of angle! The room looks completely bright, normal, and transparent.

4. What Can It Cloak (and What Can't It)?

Object / Display Light Type Does Casper Cloak It?
LCD Computer Monitors & Laptops Linearly Polarized ✓ YES — Turns Pitch Black
LED / LCD Conference Room TVs Linearly Polarized ✓ YES — Turns Pitch Black
Whiteboards & Paper Notes Unpolarized (Diffuse) ✕ NO — 100% Clearly Visible
People, Chairs & Office Furniture Unpolarized (Diffuse) ✕ NO — 100% Clearly Visible
OLED Displays (some phones/TVs) Unpolarized (Direct organic LEDs) ✕ NO — Does not use LCD polarizers
Try This in Real Life with Polarized Sunglasses!
You don't need expensive architectural film to test this! Put on a pair of polarized sunglasses, look at your computer monitor or smartphone, and tilt your head 90 degrees. The screen will suddenly turn pitch black—the exact same physics behind Casper Cloaking Film!
