Colors & the Spectrum of Light

All light is made of the same electromagnetic waves. The only difference is wavelength. Human eyes can only see a tiny rainbow sliver (380–750 nm)—everything else is invisible to us!

2. The Visible Colors: The Rainbow Sliver (380 nm – 750 nm)

This is the only light human eye cone cells can detect. Red has the longest wavelength and lowest energy; Violet has the shortest wavelength and highest energy!

530 nm — Green

🔴 Red Light (~620 – 750 nm)

Lowest energy visible light (~1.8 eV). Scatters the least in air, which is why brake lights and stop signs are red!

🟣 Violet Light (~380 – 450 nm)

Highest energy visible light (~3.1 eV). Scatters easily in the atmosphere and sits right on the edge of invisible Ultraviolet.

3. The 6 Types of Invisible Light

Light that human eyes cannot see, arranged from longest wavelength to shortest:

Type of Light Wavelength Size Why It Is Invisible Everyday Example
1. Radio Waves Kilometers to meters (building size) Energy too low to activate eye cells Wi-Fi, cellphones, FM radio
2. Microwaves Centimeters to millimeters Spins water molecules to heat food Microwave ovens, radar, Bluetooth
3. Infrared (Heat) Millimeters to 750 nm Emitted by warm objects and bodies TV remotes, thermal night vision cameras
4. Ultraviolet (UV) 380 nm down to 10 nm Blocked by the eye's cornea/lens Sunlight tanning/sunburn, blacklights
5. X-Rays 10 nm down to 0.01 nm (atom size) High energy; passes through soft tissue Medical bone scans, airport security
6. Gamma Rays Under 0.01 nm (nucleus size) Extremely high energy; deeply penetrating Nuclear power, cancer radiotherapy, space supernovae

4. Why Can't Humans See Invisible Light?

Two Physical Reasons:
• Too Weak (Radio, Microwave, Infrared): Each photon carries less than 1.8 eV of energy. That is not enough energy to trigger the 11-cis-retinal molecular switch in your eye's photoreceptors.

• Too Strong (UV, X-Ray, Gamma): High-energy photons would destroy fragile retina cells! To protect your vision, your eye's cornea and crystalline lens absorb UV rays before they can reach the retina (though bees and butterflies can see UV!).
